2. Fitbod

⭐⭐⭐⭐½ 8.5/10

Fitbod has been around since 2015 and has a polished, intuitive interface. Its AI generates individual workouts based on your recovery status and available equipment. It tracks muscle group fatigue and rotates exercises accordingly.

The downside: Fitbod generates individual workouts, not structured programs. There's no mesocycle, no deload weeks, and progressive overload relies on you manually adding weight. It's a good reactive tool, but it won't proactively push your progression like REPMAX does.

✅ Pros

  • Beautiful UI
  • Smart muscle recovery tracking
  • Equipment-aware
  • Apple Watch support

❌ Cons

  • No mesocycle programming
  • Free plan is only 3 workouts
  • $12.99/month premium
  • No social features

3. Jefit

⭐⭐⭐⭐ 8.0/10

Jefit is a veteran app with a huge exercise database and a strong social community. It offers workout planning tools and has recently added AI-powered features like the "Strength Engine" and "Movement Balance" scores.

Jefit excels as a tracker and community platform but its AI programming isn't as sophisticated as purpose-built options. The interface can feel cluttered compared to newer apps.

✅ Pros

  • Massive exercise database
  • Social community features
  • NSPI scoring system
  • Free plan available

❌ Cons

  • Cluttered interface
  • AI is add-on, not core
  • No periodization

4. Alpha Progression

⭐⭐⭐⭐ 8.0/10

Alpha Progression is popular in the bodybuilding community for its science-based approach. It uses RIR (Reps in Reserve) to automate intensity and generates structured programs focused on hypertrophy.

If you're specifically into bodybuilding-style training with precise volume tracking, Alpha Progression delivers. But it lacks the social features and diet tracking that make all-in-one apps more convenient.

5. JuggernautAI

⭐⭐⭐⭐½ 8.5/10

The gold standard for competitive powerlifters. JuggernautAI provides incredibly precise programming for squat, bench, and deadlift with sophisticated fatigue management. If you're preparing for a powerlifting meet, this is your app.

But for general gym-goers looking for hypertrophy or mixed training, it's overkill and expensive. No free plan, and the interface is narrowly focused on the Big 3 lifts.

How We Tested

We evaluated each app across 5 criteria:

  1. Programming quality: Does the AI understand periodization, progressive overload, and deload programming?

  5. Split support: Can it handle PPL, Upper/Lower, 5/3/1, and custom splits?
